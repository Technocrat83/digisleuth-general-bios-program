## Scientific separation

Observation acquires evidence under its sampling contract. Adjudication determines whether a proposed transition is permitted. Actuation changes the environment. An execution receipt documents an invocation; an independent postcondition witness tests its effect.

A real quantum Zeno experiment reports inhibition of transitions under defined physical measurement interventions. That result does not establish the clip's proposed connection between mental intention and desired macroscopic outcomes, nor causal equivalence with software feedback control.

## Proposed causal contrasts — not an executable protocol

A future frozen protocol should distinguish:

1. **A — baseline:** authorized task execution without the proposed runtime feedback mechanism.
2. **B — monitoring:** the same task with invariant monitoring but without automatic corrective actuation.
3. **C — governed correction:** the same monitoring with separately authorized, bounded correction.

An independent external witness records outcomes in every arm; removing runtime monitoring must never remove the measurement required to evaluate the comparison. Define matched disturbance schedules, allocation/blinding, comparable budgets, and monitoring overhead before trial. Differences between A and B investigate monitoring and its interference; differences between B and C investigate correction. These comparisons do not alone prove necessity, sufficiency, or universal constitutiveness.

Separate, targeted ablations may address intent persistence (C02/C05), planning (C03), and effect verification (C07/C08). Ablations must avoid substituting implementation failure for removal of the claimed functional role.
